Editorial Policy

The standards every Hourvia guide is held to.

Readers rely on Hourvia for clear, accurate information. These standards guide everything we publish.

00:00

Independence

We do not accept payment, free products or gifts in exchange for coverage, and our guides are brand-neutral. Advertisers, if present, have no influence over our content.

This matters more than it sounds, because so much online writing about these subjects exists to sell something. We take no payment, samples or sponsorship in exchange for a mention, we carry no affiliate links, and nobody can pay to appear on hourvia.com or to be left out of it. Any advertising is served by a third party and sits clearly apart from our own writing. The practical result is that when we say something is worth doing, the only reason is that we think it is.

01:00

Research

We draw on textile references, fibre and care-labelling information, and the experience of dressmakers and everyday wearers. Technical facts are checked against recognised sources.

In practice that means reading the published material properly rather than summarising somebody else’s summary, checking a claim in more than one place before repeating it, and writing down what we are unsure about. Where guidance differs between sources, we say so instead of picking the most convenient version. We revisit pages when something changes, and the date at the top of a guide tells you when it was last looked at. Where the right answer depends on your own circumstances, we say that too.

02:00

Responsible writing

  • We describe how fabrics generally behave rather than making absolute claims.
  • We do not promote specific brands or products.
  • We explain technical terms in plain language.
  • We note where personal preference and fit matter most.

We try to write the way we would explain something to a friend: plainly, in order, without jargon and without pretending to more certainty than we have. Where a question genuinely has no single right answer, we say what it depends on instead of picking one and sounding confident. We avoid scare tactics, invented urgency and exaggerated claims. If a subject could affect somebody’s health, safety or money, we say clearly when it is time to stop reading and ask a qualified person instead.

03:00

Corrections

If you spot an error, please tell us through our contact page. We review every report, correct confirmed mistakes promptly and update the page date for significant changes.

Mistakes are inevitable in anything written at length, and the only question is how quickly they are fixed. Reader corrections go straight to the front of the queue, and we would rather hear about a small error than leave it sitting on the page. Where a correction changes the meaning of a guide, we update the page and its date rather than editing quietly. Write to hello@hourvia.com with the page and what looks wrong, and you will get a reply from a person.

04:00

Images and tools

Photographs are stock images described with accurate alternative text. Interactive tools are created by us and offer general guidance only.

Photographs are stock images that illustrate the subject rather than document a specific item or result, and each one carries alternative text describing what it shows. The interactive tools are written by us, run in your browser and are based on typical values rather than on your own measurements, so they are a starting point and not a verdict. We test them before publishing and fix them when a reader reports something odd. Neither the images nor the tools are supplied or sponsored by anybody else.

05:00

What we will not publish

We do not invent reviews, testimonials or statistics, we do not publish numbers we cannot stand behind, and we do not present an opinion as a tested fact. If we have not checked something ourselves, we say where the information comes from.

We also will not publish a guide on something we have not actually looked into, pad a page out to make it rank, or write a recommendation we would not follow ourselves. If a question has no good answer yet, we would rather say so than fill the space. When we are drawing on somebody else’s work rather than our own experience, we say where it came from so you can check it yourself.

06:00

Plain language

We write in plain words and explain a technical term the first time it appears. Where an answer is genuinely “it depends”, we say what it depends on instead of giving false certainty.

Plain does not mean shallow. We would rather spend an extra sentence explaining why something happens than leave you with a rule to memorise, because understanding the reason is what lets you adapt it to your own situation. Where a technical term is genuinely the right word, we use it and define it once. If a passage on hourvia.com is hard to follow, that is our fault and worth telling us about.

07:00

Accessibility

We aim to keep hourvia.com readable for everyone: meaningful alternative text on images, headings in order, labels on every form field, and enough colour contrast to read comfortably. If something on the site is hard to use, please tell us.

These are not extras we add at the end; they are part of how the pages are built. Text resizes without breaking the layout, every tool can be used with a keyboard, and the pages are readable on a phone without pinching and dragging. We test at narrow and wide screen sizes before publishing. If something on hourvia.com is still awkward with a screen reader or at a large text size, please tell us what you were using and we will fix it.

08:00

How we choose what to cover

We write about the questions readers actually ask us, and about the points where good information is hardest to find. We do not choose topics because a brand or an advertiser would like us to.

The questions that reach our inbox are the best guide we have, and most of what is on hourvia.com started life as one. We also cover the points where the usual advice is confidently wrong, because correcting those is more useful than repeating what is already everywhere. We do not chase topics simply because they are popular, and we leave out subjects we are not in a position to write about properly.

09:00

Sources we rely on

We work from published reference material, from manufacturers’ own technical and care guidance, and from ordinary practical use. Where a subject is genuinely uncertain, or where the right answer depends on your own situation, we say so rather than inventing a rule.

Where we can check something ourselves, we do, and we say that is what we did. Where we are relying on published material or a manufacturer’s own guidance, we say that instead of presenting it as first-hand experience. If two reputable sources disagree, the honest thing is to tell you they disagree and explain what the disagreement turns on, which is usually more useful than a confident single answer.

10:00

Reviewing and updating

Pages carry the date they were last updated. We revisit guides when standards or products change, when a reader tells us something has moved on, or when we notice a gap. Older material stays available with its date visible rather than being quietly rewritten.

A guide is not finished when it is published. We go back to pages when the subject moves, when a reader tells us something has changed, or when we spot an error ourselves, and we change the date at the top when we do. We do not quietly rewrite the past, and we do not bump dates to look busy. If a page has not been reviewed for a while, the date says so honestly.

11:00

Advertising and editorial are kept apart

Any advertising on hourvia.com is served by a third party and is clearly distinct from our own writing. Advertisers do not see articles before they are published and have no say in what we publish or how we describe anything.

No advertiser sees an article before publication, suggests a topic, or has any way to have something removed. Ads are served by a third party and sit in clearly separate blocks so you can tell at a glance what is ours and what is not. We also do not accept gifts, samples or paid placements, which keeps the question from arising in the first place. If an ad on hourvia.com looks misleading, tell us and we will raise it.

12:00

Feedback from readers

Reader corrections are the fastest way we find mistakes, and they go to the front of the queue. Write to hello@hourvia.com and tell us the page and what looks wrong.

Tell us the page, the part that looks wrong and, if you can, what you think it should say. You do not need to be polite about it and you do not need to be certain. Corrections are read by a person and dealt with ahead of everything else, and you will get a reply. Suggestions for subjects we should cover are just as welcome, and a fair number of the guides on hourvia.com exist because somebody asked.
