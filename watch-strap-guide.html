Watch Strap Guide

A new strap is the quickest way to change the character of a watch. Here is what each material is good at and how to fit it properly.

12 o’clock

12:00

Find your lug width

Lug width is the gap between the lugs where the strap fits, measured in millimetres. It is often 18, 20 or 22 mm and is usually listed in the watch's specifications. If not, measure it carefully with a ruler or calipers. A strap must match it exactly to sit securely.

03:00

Leather

Leather suits dress and everyday watches and softens to your wrist over time. Keep it away from water and sweat where you can, let it dry naturally if it gets wet, and rotate it with another strap in hot weather so it lasts longer.

06:00

Metal bracelets

Steel and titanium bracelets are hard-wearing and suit sports watches.

  • Remove or add links so the bracelet sits snugly without pinching.
  • A micro-adjust clasp lets you loosen it slightly on warm days.
  • Clean between links with a soft brush and warm, soapy water if the watch is water resistant.
09:00

Rubber and fabric

Rubber straps are ideal for swimming and sport: they are comfortable when wet and easy to rinse. Fabric and woven nylon straps are light, breathable and cheap to swap, which makes them a favourite for field and military-style watches.

12:00

Changing a strap

Most straps are held by spring bars. Use a spring bar tool to compress the bar from the side, ease the strap out, and fit the new one the same way. Work over a soft cloth so the watch cannot be scratched, and check the bar has clicked into both holes before wearing it.
