Watch Size Guide

A watch that fits looks right and feels right. Here is how to read the numbers on a specification sheet and match them to your wrist.

12 o’clock

12:00

Measure your wrist

Wrap a soft tape measure, or a strip of paper you can measure afterwards, around your wrist just below the bone. Note the figure in centimetres. Most adult wrists measure between 15 and 20 cm, and that one number is the best starting point for every other choice.

03:00

Case diameter

Case diameter is measured across the case, usually without the crown. As a rough guide:

  • Wrists of 15 to 16 cm: 34 to 38 mm.
  • Wrists of 16 to 18 cm: 38 to 41 mm.
  • Wrists of 18 to 20 cm: 40 to 44 mm.

Dress watches tend to look best at the smaller end, sports and dive watches at the larger end.

06:00

Lug to lug

The distance from the tip of the top lugs to the tip of the bottom lugs often matters more than the diameter. If the lugs reach past the edges of your wrist, the watch will overhang and the strap will gap. Look at your wrist from above: the lugs should sit inside its width.

09:00

Thickness and weight

A watch under about 10 mm thick slips under a shirt cuff easily. Dive watches and chronographs are often 12 to 15 mm. A heavy steel watch on a small wrist tends to slide around, so a lighter case in titanium, or a fabric strap, can feel more balanced.

12:00

Try it on

Numbers only go so far. If you can, try a watch on, or hold a paper cut-out of the case at the right size against your wrist. Look in a mirror at arm's length, the way other people will see it, and notice how it feels after a few minutes.
